## 1. Iris Desk Voice  (0:00–0:12)
*On screen:* title slide

This is Iris Desk Voice, an open source project by Wyndham Heaven at elghaly. Iris is a calm, phone-first helper for one question: what just happened? This narration is an AI voice.

## 2. iris-35.elghaly.dev  (0:12–0:24)
*On screen:* desk slide with real screenshot site-desktop.png

Iris is one static page at iris-35 dot elghaly dot dev. It asks: What happened? You tap your situation, like: I signed or sent crypto.

## 3. Honest limits, first  (0:24–0:38)
*On screen:* phone slide with real screenshot m-what.png

First, the page says plainly what Iris can and cannot do. It cannot recover funds, reverse a transaction, or say something is safe. If money moved, go to your exchange, bank, or police.

## 4. Step 1 · The phone desk  (0:38–0:54)
*On screen:* phone slide with real screenshot m-iphone.png, m-android.png

Step one: the phone desk. Pick iPhone or Android, and answer no, yes, or not sure. Is there a device you do not know? A profile you did not install? The page classes the phone A, B or C, and tells you what to remove yourself.

## 5. Step 2 · The chain read  (0:54–1:12)
*On screen:* phone slide with real screenshot m-chain-transfer.png

Step two: the chain read. Paste a public Solana signature, a Bitcoin or Ethereum hash, or an explorer link. This real mainnet transfer shows the program, the zero point one SOL transfer, and exact balance changes, fee included. Class A: quiet.

## 6. What was actually signed  (1:12–1:25)
*On screen:* phone slide with real screenshot m-chain-approve.png

This real transaction is not quiet. Class C: act now. Two unknown programs, an unlimited token approval to a delegate, and a SetAuthority change, all behind one tap.

## 7. Known programs, nothing hidden  (1:25–1:36)
*On screen:* phone slide with real screenshot m-chain-jupiter.png

A real Jupiter swap reads Class B. Jupiter and Pump programs are on the allowlist, but an approval or authority change inside a swap is still shown, never hidden.

## 8. No secrets · Ask IRIS  (1:36–1:51)
*On screen:* phone slide with real screenshot m-seed.png, m-ask.png

Iris never wants secrets. A pasted seed phrase is rejected before any network call, and the box is wiped. Ask IRIS matches what the caller said, offline. They need my code? Do not send the code. Hang up.

## 9. Step 3 · The voice desk  (1:51–2:08)
*On screen:* desk slide with real screenshot voice-desktop.png

Step three: the voice desk, built on the AssemblyAI Voice Agent API. Tap Connect and talk, and ask out loud. When a question matches a topic, like the phone check or the chain read, the agent calls a show link tool and highlights that card on screen.

## 10. Locked desk rules  (2:08–2:19)
*On screen:* desk slide with real screenshot voice-desktop2.png

Its rules are locked on the server. It never accepts seed phrases, private keys or passwords, gives no financial advice, and points people to human support.

## 11. How it works  (2:19–2:32)
*On screen:* bullets slide

How it works: plain HTML and JavaScript under a strict content security policy. The only outbound calls are public chain RPCs. It opens offline, in six languages.

## 12. Open source · github.com/Alarm2024/iris-35  (2:32–2:44)
*On screen:* desk slide with real screenshot github-decoder.png

It is open source at github dot com slash Alarm2024 slash iris-35, with fifty-four tests on six real Solana mainnet fixtures, and exact BigInt balance math.

## 13. Principles  (2:44–2:59)
*On screen:* bullets slide

Iris is read-only and dry by default: it never signs, sends or approves. No keys, no funds, no custody. Software you can run yourself. Iris Desk Voice is still building. Thanks for watching.
